# Classical standards-engineering methodology for deriving conformance test suites from natural-language standards

Scope note: every primary source below was retrieved and read in full text (ETSI "deliver" PDFs and the ITU-T free Recommendation PDF). Where a figure comes only from a search-engine summary rather than a document I opened, it is flagged as **unverified**.

The genre the official ISO/IEC TS 18013-6 suite belongs to has an exact name in this tradition: it is a **TSS&TP** — a Test Suite Structure and Test Purposes specification — standardised by **ISO/IEC 9646-2 clause 10**, published verbatim as **ITU-T Recommendation X.291 (04/1995)**, which is freely downloadable. Everything below is organised around that.

## Q1. ISO/IEC 9646: what exactly are TSS&TP, the ATS, the ICS and the IXIT, and are the derivation rules automatable?

### Takeaway
ISO/IEC 9646-2 (= ITU-T X.291) defines a four-artefact chain — ICS proforma → TSS&TP → ATS → IXIT — and its clause 10 contains an explicitly enumerated derivation procedure: a mandatory 17-category test-group checklist (10.2.1), a one-TP-per-conformance-requirement rule (10.3.1, 10.3.5), and a clause-by-clause **minimum adequate coverage ladder** (10.4) that is essentially the same ladder as presence/encoding/value/boundary/combination. The structural part is rule-governed enough to mechanise; the standard itself says the *adequacy* of the result is not measurable, and ETSI says going from requirements to test purposes is "an empirical job".

### Cited Findings

Artefact definitions and chain:
- The conformance testing specification for a base specification comprises, as logical components: the ICS proforma; the **TSS&TP**; the Abstract Test Method (ATM); ATS conventions; the **ATS**; Test Co-ordination Procedures; a **partial IXIT proforma**; a PCTR (Protocol Conformance Test Report) proforma; a list of **untestable TPs**; abstract selection rules; and an ATS-to-TP map — ETSI's checklist mapping onto ISO/IEC 9646-2 clause 14.1 — [ETSI ETS 300 406 (April 1995), clause 7 and Table 1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- "The test suite structure and set of test purposes applicable to all ATSs to be specified for the same base specification … shall be specified in the relevant conformance testing specification, **preferably in a separate part**." — [ITU-T X.291 = ISO/IEC 9646-2, clause 10.1.1](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-X.291-199504-I!!PDF-E&type=items)
- "Each ATS shall comprise a number of test cases, **each of which is designed to achieve one of the specified test purposes**. The test cases may be grouped into test groups, if necessary nested. The structure shall be hierarchical" — X.291 clause 10.1.2. ETSI tightens this: "**Each ATC shall always relate to one and only one TP** (or CTP)" — [ETS 300 406, clause 7.4.10](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- A test purpose is "a prose description of a well-defined objective of testing, focusing on a single conformance requirement or a set of related conformance requirements as specified in the base specification" — [ETSI ETR 266 (Aug 1996), clause 3.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_etr/200_299/266/01_60/etr_266e01p.pdf), extending the ISO/IEC 9646-1 definition from "the appropriate OSI specification"
- The TSS "reflects the coverage of the reference specification by the TS: it is a synopsis of 'which tests are performed on which aspects of the reference specification'. The conformance requirements and the ICS proforma … are an essential source of cross-reference to check that the coverage of the test suite specified by the TSS&TP is acceptable." — [ETS 300 406, clause 7.4.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- IXIT: the PIXIT answers, "just as the PICS answers, are used in the **test case selection expressions**" — ETS 300 406, clause 6.6 footnote 7. Whether a mandatory sending capability can actually be *observed* "depends on the test method … and on the implementor's choices on the SUT. **It is documented in the IXIT**" — ETS 300 406, clause 6.4
- In modern practice the selection machinery lives in the ATS: "In the normal situation, TTCN IS is used, and **selection expressions are part of the ATS**" (test case selection expression table) — ETS 300 406, clause 7.4.9

The derivation procedure (X.291 clause 10.2–10.3):
- The specifier "shall ensure that the test suite includes test cases for whichever of the following categories are relevant", listing **seventeen categories a)–q)**: a) capability tests (for static conformance requirements); b) valid behaviour; c) invalid test events, subdivided into **syntactically invalid, semantically invalid, and inopportune**; d) different roles of the IUT; e) PDUs sent to the IUT; f) PDUs received from the IUT; g) interactions between PDUs sent and received; h) each mandatory capability; i) each optional capability; j) each protocol phase; k) variations in the test event occurring in a particular state; l) timing and timer variations; m) PDU encoding variations; n) variations in values of individual parameters; o) variations in combinations of parameter values; p) combinations of related requirements from more than one base specification; q) multi-party behaviour. "This list is not exhaustive … Furthermore, these categories **overlap one another** and it is the task of the test suite specifier to arrange them into an appropriate hierarchical structure." — [X.291 clause 10.2.1](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-X.291-199504-I!!PDF-E&type=items)
- Clause 10.2.2 gives a concrete four-level example tree: **A Capability tests** (A.1 mandatory, A.2 optional capabilities); **B valid behaviour**, substructured by protocol phase (B.1 connection establishment, B.2 data transfer, B.3 release) × focus (B.x.1 what is sent to the IUT, B.x.2 what the IUT is requested to send, B.x.3 interactions) × aspect (.1 test event variation in each state, .2 timing/timer variation, .3 encoding variation, .4 individual parameter value variation, .5 combination of parameter values); **C syntactically/semantically invalid behaviour**; **D inopportune events** — X.291 clause 10.2.2
- Basic interconnection tests "may be provided as a **list of selected capability and/or behaviour tests, but shall not involve any additional test purposes**" — X.291 clause 10.2.3. (This is the published precedent for the 27 "perform all Common_X test cases" reuse-by-reference entries in TS 18013-6.)
- "The test purposes may be produced directly from studying those clauses in the relevant specification(s) which are appropriate to the test group concerned. For some test groups, the test purposes **may be derivable directly from the protocol state table; for others, they may be derivable from the PDU encoding definitions or the descriptions of particular parameters, or from text which specifies the relevant conformance requirements**." — X.291 clause 10.3.1
- Suggested working order: identify test groups first, write a **test group objective** for each, then refine objectives into nested objectives or individual TPs. "**This orderly construction technique helps to ensure the adequate coverage** … It also avoids unnecessary duplication of text in the test purposes, because the full description of each test purpose does not have to be written explicitly, but **can be assembled by tracing a path down through the nested structure of test group objectives**." — X.291 clause 10.3.1
- "There shall be **at least one test purpose related to each distinct conformance requirement** or set of related conformance requirements." — X.291 clause 10.3.5
- "Test purposes should be specified not only for clearly testable conformance requirements, but also for conformance requirements that may be **untestable** using the test methods defined … NOTE – Test purposes for untestable requirements serve to inform the specifiers of the base specifications which conformance requirements are untestable, **by indicating gaps in the ATSs**." — X.291 clause 10.3.6
- Two-stage combination rule: identify TPs per individual conformance requirement first; then TPs for combinations may be written, which must reference the superseded individual TPs, must declare that one abstract test case is produced for the combination, and each superseded TP "shall remain in the set of test purposes, but shall identify the new test purpose(s) which supersede it" — X.291 clause 10.3.3
- Hard constraint on mixing: "the testing of **invalid parameter values shall not be combined** with the testing of other valid or invalid values in a single test purpose" — X.291 clause 10.3.2
- On automation: "NOTE – If the test suite specifier employs a **formal description** of the base specification(s) concerned, test purposes may be derived from that **by means of some automated method**. If an automated method is used, the same requirements apply. However, methods based on Formal Description Techniques (FDTs) are **outside the scope** of this Recommendation." — X.291 clause 10.3.1

The coverage ladder (X.291 clause 10.4) — "a minimum 'adequate' coverage":
- a) capability groups: "at least one test purpose per relevant capability" and "at least one test purpose per relevant PDU type and each major variation of each such type, using 'normal' or default values for each parameter"
- b) state/event groups: "at least one test purpose per relevant state/event combination"
- c) timers: "at least one test purpose concerned with the **expiry of each defined timer**"
- d) encoding: "at least one test purpose for each relevant kind of **encoding variation** per relevant PDU type"
- e) valid individual parameter values: for each relevant **integer** parameter, TPs for "the **boundary values and one randomly selected mid-range value**"; for each **bitwise** parameter, "as many values as practical, but not less than all the 'normal' or common values"; for other parameters, "at least one test purpose concerned with a value different from what is considered 'normal' or default"
- f) invalid individual parameter values: for each integer parameter, "invalid values **adjacent to the allowed boundary values** defined in the base specification, plus one other randomly selected invalid value"; for bitwise parameters, "as many invalid values as practical"; "for all other relevant types of parameter, at least one test purpose per parameter"
- g) combinations of parameter values: "at least one test purpose for each important combination of specific values (e.g. boundary values)" and "at least one test purpose per set of interrelated parameters to test an arbitrary combination of relevant values"
- ICS-gating rule inside 10.4: "The test suite shall include test cases to check for the support of parameter values that are allowed by the base specification(s) and are **within the ranges stated in the ICS** … Parameter values that are **valid with respect to the base specification(s) but outside the ranges stated in the ICS shall not be tested**." Note 2 adds that invalid-value tests "should focus on values outside the range defined in the relevant base specification, rather than valid values outside the range claimed in the ICS."
- Explicit refusal to endorse a coverage metric: "NOTE 3 – The progression of the work on formal methods in conformance testing **may provide analytical approaches** to assess the appropriate coverage of an ATS, especially for the state/event variations … This Recommendation, however, **does not recommend any particular analytical approach**." — X.291 clause 10.4
- The TSS&TP part must carry a **compliance clause** requiring that a conforming ATS "consists of a set of test cases corresponding to the set or to a subset of the test purposes specified in the TSS&TP part" — X.291 clause 10.5
- Accountability obligations: "The test suite specifier **shall provide an explanation of how the test purposes are derived from or relate to the base specification**. The test suite specifier shall also provide a **summary of the coverage achieved** by the ATS." — X.291 clause 10.1.3

### Inferences
- The 9646-2 clause 10.4 ladder is the direct historical ancestor of the tool's deterministic coverage-item enumeration (presence/absence, encoding valid/invalid, value valid/invalid, boundary min/max/outside, combination). The tool is re-deriving, from ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-4, a ladder that 9646-2 published in 1991/1995 as the *definition of minimum adequacy for a conformance suite*. That is a strong citable claim: the enumeration stage is not novel, it is the classical rule set, and it was always intended to be applied mechanically clause-by-clause.
- 9646-2 is unusually automation-friendly in three specific places: 10.2.1's category list is a fixed taxonomy; 10.3.5 is a hard cardinality rule (≥1 TP per distinct requirement); and 10.4 is a per-datatype enumeration rule. What 9646-2 does *not* mechanise is the step the tool also does not mechanise well: arranging overlapping categories into a hierarchy (10.2.1 last sentence) and deciding which items are "relevant".
- 10.3.6 ("test purposes for untestable requirements … indicating gaps") is the published precedent for the tool's "unproducible items become recorded gaps". Worth citing directly: recording gaps is standard-mandated behaviour, not a workaround.

### Gaps
- ISO/IEC 9646-7 (ICS proforma requirements), the part that specifies proforma structure, is mirrored as ITU-T X.296 but is **not** in ITU's free-download set (three id variants returned HTML, not PDF). Its contents are reported here only indirectly through ETS 300 406 Annex A, which prescribes criteria "in addition to ISO/IEC 9646-7 criteria".
- I could not retrieve ISO/IEC 9646-1 (X.290) clause text on IXIT proforma contents; the IXIT findings above come from ETS 300 406's citations of it.

## Q2. How ETSI and 3GPP actually produce suites today, and whether requirement→test-purpose mapping is published guidance

### Takeaway
Yes — ETSI publishes an explicit, staged pipeline and two generations of guidance for it: ETR 266 (1996, the Test Purpose style guide, with an eight-step "starting from the requirements" algorithm) and EG 203 130 (2013, the model-based-testing methodology, which specifies a **requirements catalogue** with per-requirement attributes including a modal-verb-based Modality field). The 2005–06 ETSI IPv6 project is the published case study of running this pipeline on RFCs, and it reports requirement and test counts.

### Cited Findings

The canonical ETSI pipeline (EG 203 130 process figure, clause 6):
- Base Standard Specification → **Identification of Requirements** → **Requirements Catalogue** → **Creation of ICS/IFS** → **Definition of Test Suite Structure (TSS)** → **Specification of Test Purposes (TPs)** → **Specification of Test Descriptions (TDs)** → **Specification of Test Cases (TCs)**, with an MBT branch of Modelling for Testing → Test Generation → Transformation & Adaptation → Generated Test Cases, and Validation feeding back — [ETSI EG 203 130 V1.1.1 (2013-04), clause 5/6 process figure](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_eg/203100_203199/203130/01.01.01_60/eg_203130v010101p.pdf)
- "The 'Cataloguing of Requirements' comprises the identification of requirements **by analysis of the base standard**. It is recommended to collect and structure the identified requirements in a catalogue." — EG 203 130 clause 5
- Requirements-catalogue rules, EG 203 130 clause 6.1: each requirement "is usually an **excerpt** (most often, textual …) **from the standard text, containing usually one specific statement** on the conforming system behaviour"; "**implicit requirements** implied by the standard text … are made explicit and put in the catalogue"; "each requirement has a unique identifier … it is recommended that requirement identifier contains information on **source document**"; requirements may be grouped in tree-like hierarchies; "requirements stating effectively the same should be **united in a special group** … provided with the most precise statement expressing them all"; "**applicability context** of each requirement — the full set of conditions when this requirement is applicable — should be specified explicitly"
- Mandated per-requirement **attributes** (EG 203 130 clause 6.1): **Role(s) to apply to**; **Modality** — "obligatory (specified using words like 'shall', 'must', 'always', 'definitely', 'never', 'required', etc.), recommended ('should', 'ought to', 'recommended') or optional ('may', 'might', 'possible', 'permitted')"; **Testability** — "testable requirement has a definite finite procedure, which allows an arbitrary adequately experienced person to determine whether this requirement holds or not. Non-testable requirements are sometimes admissible." Conditional dependencies ("each time an optional requirement A holds, a requirement B should also hold") "should also be extracted from the standard and clearly stated in the catalogue", as should relations such as "one requirement refines another, formulates alternative behaviour, is similar but differs in some detail"
- ETR 266's "starting from the requirements" eight-step algorithm (clause 4.3.1.2), explicitly the **preferred** of three methods: 1) identify all the conformance requirements of the reference specification; 2) identify the PICS entry(ies) corresponding to them; 3) structure the conformance requirements into nested homogeneous groups, giving a first TSS; 4) compare that draft TSS with the 9646-2 guidance and identify gaps; 5) define the TPs corresponding to the requirements or sets of requirements identified; 6) feed the TSS tree with these TPs; 7) refine TSS and TPs, iteratively; 8) check the coverage of the test suite. "**This way of proceeding is preferred** … since it starts from the conformance requirements. The TSS structure applicable to a given standard **should not be defined a priori**, but should be the result of the process of conformance requirements analysis." — [ETR 266 clause 4.3.1.2 and Figure 1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_etr/200_299/266/01_60/etr_266e01p.pdf)
- ETR 266's candid reason for existing: "the ETSI groups developing Test Purposes are generally unfamiliar with conformance testing matters. **A very open standard like ISO/IEC 9646 leaves a lot of freedom and gives little practical guidance**" — ETR 266, Introduction

Model-based testing standards:
- ETSI ES 202 951 specifies the concepts a modelling notation must support so that "model-based testing tools that use a modelling notation that complies with the requirements stated in the present document **can be used to automatically generate abstract test cases suitable for standardization**"; the generated cases must "follow the principles of ISO/IEC 9646-1 … as, for example, put forward in the TTCN-3 test suites" — [ETSI ES 202 951 V1.1.1 (2011-07), clause 1 and Introduction](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_es/202900_202999/202951/01.01.01_60/es_202951v010101p.pdf)
- ES 202 951's stated motivation is automating **test design**, not test execution: "Contrary to other methods and approaches, which focus mainly on automation of test execution, the present document considers the use of model-based testing for the **automation of test design**." — ES 202 951, Introduction
- EG 203 130 clause 7.1 defines coverage criteria for generated suites, first among them **requirements coverage**: "A situation corresponds to realization of the **applicability conditions of some requirement**. The full set of situations corresponds to the full set of requirements. This is so called requirements coverage criterion." Others listed: transition coverage, statement coverage, input-data-partitioning coverage, mutation-based coverage; and "several coverage criteria can be combined in a mixed one" whose situation set is the union — EG 203 130 clause 7.1
- EG 203 130 also records that unreachable situations are excluded from the denominator: "unreachable situations are removed and test coverage achieved is calculated using only the set of reachable situations. This rule ensures that **less than 100 % coverage according to a criterion always means that some reachable behaviour is not covered**" — EG 203 130 clause 7.1
- EG 203 130 clause 6.2 catalogues what defeats mechanical derivation from prose: ambiguity ("possibility of different interpretations of the standard text"), inability to design a clear checking procedure for a requirement marked testable, incompleteness, inconsistencies between requirement statements, and "lack of reasonable integrity between different parts of the standard, when very similar things are represented differently, the same procedure is described several times in different sections" — which "usually means that the standard text is not mature"
- EG 203 130 states TPs may be written "in the dedicated language TPlan ES 202 553 or in plain text", and that a TP should carry preconditions, post-conditions and the requirement under test including pass criteria, "preferably with references to the standard or to the requirements catalogues" — EG 203 130 clause 5

The ETSI IPv6 project — the published case study of applying this to RFCs (2005–2006):
- The project built a **Requirements Catalogue** from RFCs, described as "**Needed in lieu of PICS**" — implemented as "a scalable database containing all requirement elements" with a web interface offering browsing by function, user-defined search, and "links to RFC and related test specification" — [S. Randall (Vice-Chair, ETSI TC-MTS), "ETSI IPv6 Activities", 2nd ITU-T Informal Workshop on Conformance and Interoperability Testing, 8 Dec 2006, slides 6–7](https://itu.int/dms_pub/itu-t/oth/06/02/T06020000020001PDFE.pdf)
- Each catalogued requirement carries: **requirement type** (Mandatory = MUST/MUST NOT; Recommended = SHOULD/SHOULD NOT; Optional = MAY/MAY NOT), **requirement target** (Host, Router, etc.), **requirement text**, and **functional grouping** (e.g. "Process Fragmented packet", "Generate ICMPv6 Error Type") — same source, slide 7
- "This is the **first major testing project undertaken by ETSI where it has had no influence over the base protocol specifications**" — slide 3. (Directly comparable to generating tests for a standard one did not write.)
- Conformance test purposes are stated to be "on the protocol level", interoperability TPs "on the functional level"; both "Define the function being tested—the WHAT / Do not define HOW to test the function / Grouped into a logical structure (Test Suite Structure)" and both carry the explicit many-to-many rule: "**One Requirement may spawn several TPs / One TP may test several Requirements**" — slides 10 and 16

3GPP:
- ETSI TDL ES 203 119-4 Annex A.1 works a real 3GPP example, translating "the test objectives in clause 7.1.3.1 from ETSI TS 136 523-1 [3GPP TS 36.523-1] into the textual syntax for the structured test objective specification with TDL" — i.e. 3GPP UE protocol conformance specifications are structured as test objectives with Objective / Reference / Initial conditions / Expected behaviour fields — [ETSI ES 203 119-4 V1.5.1 (2022-05), Annex A.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_es/203100_203199/20311904/01.05.01_60/es_20311904v010501p.pdf)
- **Unverified (search-summary only, document not opened):** a USENIX Security paper hosted at par.nsf.gov reportedly states that in 2020, 3GPP conformance test procedures numbered 326, built by 37 technical staff coordinating about 39 companies — [par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10523016](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10523016). Treat as a lead; I did not fetch the paper.

### Inferences
- EG 203 130's requirements-catalogue specification is, item for item, the published standard-body version of the tool's requirement-extraction stage: verbatim excerpt from the standard text, one statement per requirement, unique id carrying the source document, modality derived from a modal-verb lexicon, applicability conditions made explicit, duplicates merged, relations recorded. The tool's regex-marked modal sentences plus LLM atomisation with a verbatim quote implements ETSI's own 2013 prescription. This is the strongest available claim that the extract-requirements stage is legitimately rule-governed.
- Conversely, EG 203 130 clause 6.1 demands two attributes the described pipeline does not appear to produce: **Role(s) to apply to** and **Testability**. The Role attribute is exactly what TS 18013-6's Technology tag encodes ([mDL], [DE-mDL-reader], [SR-WebAPI] …). A requirement extractor that does not assign a role/target cannot gate its coverage items the way the official suite gates its tests, which is a plausible structural contributor to a low match rate.

### Gaps
- I did not locate **ETSI EG 202 568** (named in the assignment). The ETSI deliver portal search surfaced no such document under that number in my results; the test-purpose derivation guidance I found lives in ETR 266 (1996), ETS 300 406 (1995) and EG 203 130 (2013). The report should not cite EG 202 568 without an independent check — it may be mis-numbered, withdrawn, or a different series.
- The ETSI ICS proforma style guide (ETR 212, superseded by **EG 201 058**) appeared in search results with a deliver URL but my download returned the portal's HTML challenge page rather than the PDF; I could not read its text. URL for a later attempt: `etsi.org/deliver/etsi_eg/201000_201099/201058/01.02.02_50/eg_201058v010202m.pdf`.

## Q3. The ICS proforma as a first-class artefact, and whether one could be derived automatically from a standard's M/O/C feature tables

### Takeaway
The ICS proforma is a table-per-feature-group artefact whose rows are {Item number, feature name, **Ref.** to the base-specification clause, **Status** (M / O / c*n* predicate / n/a), **Support** answer column} plus, for numeric features, an **Allowed values / Supported values** pair. ETSI publishes normative rules for which features must appear as questions and when a conditional row can be factored away — rules stated over exactly the information a standard's mandatory/optional/conditional tables contain. Nothing in the rules requires semantics beyond the tables, so an ICS proforma is in principle derivable from them; but I found **no published tool that derives one from a prose standard**.

### Cited Findings
- Two invariants of ICS status: "a status is **STATIC**, even if it is conditional … Its value is either fixed or **evaluated ONCE** as the result of a conditional expression in which other SUPPORTS are the variables, and cannot change dynamically during the functioning of the IUT. Neither status nor support ever depend on a given instance of communication"; and "an ICS question (hence, the associated status) is **related to a conformance requirement** in the reference standard" — [ETS 300 406, clause 6.4](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- The status attaches to the *requirement*, not to the feature's use: "the fact that a feature is mandatory does not mean that it will always be 'present', or 'observed'. It means that the conformance requirement has been satisfied by the implementation, and that the IUT cannot 'refuse' to exhibit the proper behaviour if it is placed in conditions where a conformance requirement from the standard requires it to do so." — ETS 300 406 clause 6.4
- "The ICS questions **shall be static conformance requirements** from the reference standard. **Dynamic questions shall not appear** in the ICS, except in particular situations" — the exception being a profile-specific ICS that adds dynamic behavioural constraints, e.g. "a profile which makes the presence of a parameter mandatory" — [ETS 300 406, Annex A.1 (normative)](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- Static vs dynamic, operationally: static = "WHAT are the requirements to conform with / WHAT capabilities need to be implemented" (examples given: capabilities/features such as link set-up; functional units such as flow control, duplex speech; protocol elements such as S_Connect_Request; parameters such as calling_addr, prot_discriminator). Dynamic = "HOW is a requirement implemented … what is the sequence of commands and actions" (example: "does implementation send a Connect_Response after receipt of a Connect_Request?") — ETS 300 406 Annex A.1
- Mandated proforma **hierarchy of question levels** (Annex A.2): roles (initiator/responder, manager/agent, DCE/DTE); major capabilities (functional units, service classes, service elements, protocol classes); negotiation capabilities; timers; PDUs; PDU parameters; then protocol error handling, multi-specification dependencies and other conditions
- The normative selection rule: "**All the roles, major capabilities, negotiation capabilities, timers, PDUs shall be subject to an ICS question, whatever their status may be: mandatory, conditional, or optional**" — with footnote "One immediate reason for this is that the PICS serves as the basis to the **static conformance review**" — ETS 300 406 Annex A.2
- The normative factoring rule for finer items: where PDU parameters' mandatory/conditional status "all directly depend on the support of a more general feature, for instance a PDU … the table related to the PDU parameters should either be **omitted, or replaced with a single question** factoring out the support of parameters: 'Are all the parameters of PDU x supported?'" — but "when in a list of PDU parameters … there is a mix of some items mandatory or conditional **directly dependent** on the support of a superior feature; and some items optional or conditional **without being directly dependent** … **the complete list of parameters should be provided**, including the optional items" — ETS 300 406 Annex A.2
- Worked table syntax (Annex A.2 examples): rows are `Item | PDU/Parameter | Ref. | Status | Support`, with predicates written as `c8 = if A3.1 then m else n/a` and an inline comment giving the predicate in prose ("i.e. if the Channel_Open_Request PDU is supported") — ETS 300 406 Annex A.2, Examples 1–4
- Numeric features get a distinct shape: "The tables specifying allowed values contain two columns for values: **allowed values, supported values**" — e.g. `Recovery_time | 7.5.2 | c.3 | | 1 – 3 600 s | ___` and `Data_Size | 7.5.6 | m | | 128, 256, 512 | ___`. Critically, "the status/support refer to the feature, and not to the allowed values. … **The implementation of the allowed values is NOT submitted to the status.**" Making particular values or intervals mandatory requires a separate table whose *items are the values/intervals themselves*, and which "shall not contain 'allowed values' or 'supported values' columns" — ETS 300 406 Annex A.3
- Why ICS consistency governs test selection: "**The test deselection process is based on the ICS and IXIT entries. An inconsistency between conformance requirements and ICS proforma may lead to the inability to deselect a test.**" Guidance 2: "Since the ICS is one of the bases of the deselection process, the analysis of the consistency between conformance requirements and ICS is an important part of the TP production process." — [ETR 266 clause 4.3.3.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_etr/200_299/266/01_60/etr_266e01p.pdf)
- The coverage criterion is stated against the ICS, not against the requirements: **Guidance 5** — "The Test specifier should check that **any mandatory or optional item of the ICS is covered by at least a TP**" (footnote: "This may be obtained through a combined TP") — ETR 266 clause 4.3.3.2
- Which makes coverage checking cheap or expensive depending on traceability: "At the end of the TSS & TP definition process, the test specifier should check the coverage … This task is **quite easy** to perform when the methodology of TP production has been followed. In this case, it is limited to a **cross-check of the TP versus the ICS entries**. On the other hand, if the information related to the relationships among test purposes and ICS entries is not … included in the test purpose, the cross-checking may be **extremely time-consuming**." — ETR 266 clause 4.3.3.2
- Deselection predicates may be arbitrarily complex: "It should be possible to determine **logically** from the ICS if it is appropriate or not to deselect the ATC corresponding to this TP", with footnote 9: "**Logically does not mean easily.** It means that by means of a logical expression, which may be complex, and which may involve a reference to several items of the PICS." — [ETS 300 406 clause 7.4.1.2](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- Combination is constrained by selection: "the combination of test purposes which may be deselected according to **different conditions** (i.e. with different selection expressions) **should be avoided**" — ETR 266 clause 4.4.1
- Profile ICS has two components: a **profile Requirement List (profile RL)** that re-uses the base specifications' ICS items, plus a **profile-specific ICS proforma** for constraints the profile adds — ETS 300 406 clauses 8.2.1–8.2.2; and the profile ICS is used for "static conformance review", "test case parameterization, de-selection", and as "components attached to test reports" — ETS 300 406 clause 8.4
- EG 203 130 places "**Creation of ICS/IFS**" as a distinct step downstream of the requirements catalogue and upstream of the TSS — [EG 203 130 clause 6 process figure](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_eg/203100_203199/203130/01.01.01_60/eg_203130v010101p.pdf)
- Nearest thing to automated ICS production that I found: **NIST ICSGenerator**, which builds an implementation conformance statement from an **XML instance of a formal information model** (the ISO/IEEE 11073-10201 Domain Information Model), driven additionally by the selected Medical Device System object type — not from prose — [NIST ICSGenerator User's Guide](https://www.nist.gov/document/icsgeneratorusersguide-v21doc)

### Inferences
- The ICS proforma row schema {Item, feature, Ref. clause, Status ∈ {M, O, c*n*}, Support} ∪ {Allowed values, Supported values} is a near-exact match for the "objects with attributes (presence mandatory/optional/conditional, type or encoding, value domain, size, relations)" that the tool's second LLM pass already extracts. The pipeline therefore already produces something isomorphic to an ICS proforma without naming it as one. Naming it, emitting it as an artefact, and keying coverage items to ICS items rather than to requirements would align the tool with ETR 266 Guidance 5, which is the *actual* published coverage criterion for this genre of suite — "every mandatory or optional ICS item covered by at least one TP", not "every sentence covered".
- Annex A.2's factoring rule is a direct, mechanisable deduplication heuristic the tool appears to lack: when every parameter of a structure inherits its status from the structure, **do not enumerate the parameters** — emit one question. Applied to coverage items, this would collapse large families of presence checks. The inverse rule (enumerate in full when statuses are mixed) is equally mechanical. 921 objects × a ladder, with no factoring rule, is a plausible mechanism for the 3257 coverage items against the official suite's 907 cases.
- Annex A.3's separation of "feature status" from "value-domain status" predicts a specific failure mode: a generator that hangs presence/absence coverage items off allowed values will produce items that no conformance suite would ever contain, because by ETSI's rule the status never governs the values.
- ETR 266's Guidance 5 plus ETS 300 406's minimum-coverage definition (below) imply that the measured 13.8 % overlap against TS 18013-6 should be re-measured as *ICS-item coverage* rather than *official-test coverage*; the two can diverge sharply because one official test may discharge several ICS items and vice versa.

### Gaps
- No published method or tool for deriving an ICS proforma from natural-language standard text was found. The two candidate sources — ISO/IEC 9646-7 / X.296, and ETSI EG 201 058 — specify proforma *structure and quality criteria*, not production; neither was retrievable in full text here. If the report wants to claim "nobody has automated ICS derivation", that claim is supported by absence of evidence across my searches, not by a source asserting it.

## Q4. Test purpose languages and formalisms — TPLan, TDL, TTCN-3 — and how close their structure is to what an LLM could fill in

### Takeaway
Both ETSI test-purpose languages impose a small, fixed slot structure that matches the TS 18013-6 record almost field for field: TPLan (ES 202 553, 2009) gives `TP id / summary / RQ ref / role / config / TC ref` + `with { preconditions } ensure that { when {stimulus} then {response} }` with a **normative EBNF grammar**, and TDL Part 4 (ES 203 119-4, 2022) gives a meta-model with an ordered set of **PICSReferences** combined by and/or plus **Initial conditions / Expected behaviour / Final conditions** compartments. Both keep free text in quoted strings, so the slots are machine-checkable while the content is not — which is exactly the shape of an LLM fill-in task.

### Cited Findings

TPLan (ETSI ES 202 553 V1.2.1, 2009-06):
- Purpose: "ISO/IEC 9646-1 and ISO/IEC 9646-2 recommend that test specifications include a concise and unambiguous description of each test which focuses on its purpose. These Test Purposes, or TPs, define **what** is to be tested rather than **how** the testing is performed. The TPs are **based on the requirements identified in the relevant standard**" — [ETSI ES 202 553 V1.2.1, clause 4.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_es/202500_202599/202553/01.02.01_60/es_202553v010201p.pdf)
- Stated benefits include "**automatic syntax checking and syntax highlighting** in text editors" and "**a basis for a TP transfer format and representation in tools**" — ES 202 553 clause 4.1
- TSS&TP composition: TSS Header (title, author, version) → Cross References (references to base standards, configuration descriptions) → Definitions (user-defined words, events, test entities, conditions, headers) → TSS Groups (possibly nested) → Test Purposes contained in the groups — ES 202 553 clause 4.3
- Complete keyword inventory (clause 5.1, Table 1), grouped: TSS header (`author date title TSS version`); cross-references (`xref`); definitions (`condition context def entity event header value unit word`); TP grouping (`end group objective`); TP header (`config id ref role RQ summary TC TD TP`); TP body (`ensure that with when then`); test entities (`IUT TESTER`); glue words (`a an as in is no of the`); logical (`and not or`); stimulus/response (`receives sends`); data-related (`containing indicating`); direction (`from to`); time- and order-related (`after before unordered within`)
- TP header slots and their status (clause 10.1): **TP id (mandatory)**; summary (optional, free text in quotes); **RQ ref** (optional, requirement identifier); **role** (optional, "the role or roles of the object being tested … e.g. router or host"); **config** (optional, reference to the test configuration); **TC ref / TD ref** (optional). User-defined header entries may be added, with the standard's own examples being `select : 'Profile A'` and `PICS ref : PICS_001, PICS_345` — ES 202 553 clauses 10.1–10.2
- TP body structure (clause 11): "Pre-conditions (optional initial conditions)" then "TP behaviour description comprising sequences of Stimuli and Responses"; the behaviour description "shall begin with the keywords **`ensure that`**"; `with { IUT 'condition 1' and 'condition 2' and not …}` "does not define the steps or actions needed to reach the starting condition, **only the condition itself**"; `when { … }` = "stimuli described from the viewpoint of the IUT", `then { … }` = "IUT responses and other behaviour"; the when/then pair "may be repeated any number of times to define a sequence of stimulus/response pairs" — ES 202 553 clauses 11.1–11.3
- Annex A is a **normative EBNF grammar** ("The TPLan Grammar": A.1 Syntactic Rules, A.2 TPLan EBNF Productions) — ES 202 553 Annex A
- Admitted limits on mechanical checking: "in order to retain expressive power, TPLan is **only loosely defined** in that no strict relation between certain words (especially the user-defined words) is specified. Thus, **it is possible to write nonsensical constructions** if care is not taken. Of course, appropriate tools may be able to identify such constructions but **there are no constructs for doing this explicitly in the notation**." — ES 202 553 clause 4.5
- A real conformance TP in TPLan, from the ETSI IPv6 suite: `TP id : TP_COR_2139_01 / summary : 'hop limit of one' / RQ Ref : RQ_001_0047 / Config : CF_COR_02 / TC Ref : TC_COR_2139_01` then `ensure that { when { IUT receives Ipv6_packet from Host containing IPv6_Header indicating Hop_limit set to 1 } then { IUT sends ICMPv6_Time_Exceeded to Host containing ICMP_code set to 0 } }` — [Randall, ITU-T workshop 2006, slide 17](https://itu.int/dms_pub/itu-t/oth/06/02/T06020000020001PDFE.pdf)

TDL structured test objectives (ETSI ES 203 119-4 V1.5.1, 2022-05):
- Scope: "specifies an extension of the Test Description Language (TDL) enabling the specification of **structured test objectives** … the necessary additional constructs in the abstract syntax, their semantics, as well as the concrete graphical syntactic notation … In addition textual syntax examples … as well as **BNF rules** for a textual syntax" — [ES 203 119-4 clause 1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_es/203100_203199/20311904/01.05.01_60/es_20311904v010501p.pdf)
- Why structured rather than prose: where plain TDL test objectives "rely on external documents or informal text", the structured approach "**enables syntactical and semantical** [checking]" and allows a domain vocabulary of entities/events to "be reused across all structured test objective specifications in that domain" — ES 203 119-4 clause 5
- The `StructuredTestObjective` meta-class carries: a description/objective; "an ordered set of **'PICSReferences' to 'PICS'**"; **Initial conditions**; **Expected behaviour**; **Final conditions** — each holding an `EventSequence` of `EventOccurrence`s. "Event occurrence specifications are organized in the different compartments of a structured test objective, including initial conditions, expected behaviour, and final conditions. Multiple event occurrences are combined by means of an [and/or] operand" — ES 203 119-4 clauses 5, 5.x
- The applicability machinery is first class: "Structured test objectives may also include **references to PICS which may be used as selection criteria** for the concrete realization of the test objectives. The PICS **shall be defined in advance as part of the domain description**. Multiple PICS references within the same structured test objective are combined by means of an 'and' or 'or' operand"; a `Comment` with body "not" negates a reference — ES 203 119-4 clause 5
- Reuse/parameterisation: "**Test objective variants** may be included in a structured test objective to describe additional test objectives derived from the structured test objective by **specialising or overriding data elements and meta information**" — ES 203 119-4 clause 5
- Domain vocabulary terms defined in clause 3.1: **context** ("set of circumstances related to the occurrence of an event"), **entity**, **entity type**, **event**, **event type**, **event occurrence** — with the note that `event` is "related to a term of the same name defined in ETSI ES 202 553 [TPLan]", i.e. TDL Part 4 is the acknowledged successor of TPLan
- Worked 3GPP example (Annex A.1), translating 3GPP TS 36.523-1 clause 7.1.3.1: `Test Purpose TP_7_1_3_1_1 / Objective: "" / Reference: "3GPP TS 36.321, clause 5.3.1" / Initial conditions with the UE entity in the "E-UTRA RRC_CONNECTED state" / Expected behaviour ensure that when the UE entity receives a "downlink assignment on the PDCCH for the UE's C-RNTI" and … then the UE entity sends a "HARQ feedback on the HARQ process"`; the negative sibling TP_7_1_3_1_2 ends `then the UE entity does not send any "HARQ feedback on the HARQ process"` — ES 203 119-4 Annex A.1
- The TDL series is split across parts: Part 1 abstract syntax and semantics, Part 2 graphical syntax, Part 3 exchange format, Part 4 structured test objectives, Part 8 textual syntax, Part 9 test runtime interfaces; there is an ETSI **TDL Open Source Project** — ES 203 119-4 clause 2 and note; [tdl.etsi.org open-source page](https://tdl.etsi.org/index.php/open-source)

ETR 266's prose template, for comparison (the pre-formal version of the same slots):
- A test purpose "should comprise the following components": **identifier (Mandatory)**, **reference to requirement (Mandatory)**, reference to selection criteria (Optional), **initial condition (Mandatory)**, **check(s) to be performed (Mandatory)**, **verdict criteria (Mandatory)**, constraints (Conditional — "mandatory in the case of test of defined values in general and parameter variation tests in particular") — [ETR 266 clause 4.2, with the component/status table](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_etr/200_299/266/01_60/etr_266e01p.pdf)
- Field-level writing rules that are checkable: the identifier "is supposed to establish the link between the Test Purpose and the relevant entry in the Test Suite Structure", and TPs "should be identified by the same referencing scheme as the Test Suite Structure"; the requirement reference "indicates the subclause of the reference standard specifications in which the conformance requirement is expressed"; the selection-criteria reference "should relate to the **PICS items** (if any) and/or the **PIXIT** details"; initial conditions "should define **observable external parameters**"; checks must be "explicit, exhaustive and unambiguous" and "**a given check can be performed in a test only if it is mentioned in the test purpose**"; verdict criteria "should explicitly describe the **observable events**" but "the purpose of this part of the TP is to describe an event and **not to assign a verdict**" — ETR 266 clauses 4.2.1–4.2.7
- The two negative-example rules most amenable to automated checking (ETR 266 clauses 4.2.4–4.2.6): a TP whose initial condition or verdict names only an internal state is defective — "Check that the IUT in state S1, on receipt of a valid connect request, enters state S2" is given as "what should not be done", because "there is no actual indication of the verdict criteria"; the fix is to name the externally observable event, "(sends a valid connect confirm)". ETS 300 406 clause 7.4.1.2 makes this normative: "The expression of a TP **shall not** be limited to checking a state or a state transition"
- For informal testing (interoperability, performance, functional, robustness, service), ETR 266 adds one component — a **test summary** — giving an eight-field "extended test purpose", "to be used in the context of informal testing when the executable test cases are **directly derived from the information contained in the test purpose**. In this case, the step of abstract test cases is skipped" — ETR 266 clauses 3.1 and 5.2

### Inferences
- Field-by-field, the TS 18013-6 record (Test case ID, System under test, Purpose, Reference, Technology, Profile, Preconditions, Test scenario, Expected results) is a TPLan/TDL test purpose with the ATS step skipped — i.e. ETR 266's **extended test purpose**, the variant defined for when executable test cases are derived directly from the TP. Mapping: Test case ID = `TP id`; System under test + Technology = `role` / TDL `PICSReference`s; Profile = `select` / further PICS reference; Purpose = `summary` / `Objective`; Reference = `RQ ref` plus base-specification subclause; Preconditions = `with { }` / `Initial conditions`; Test scenario = `when { }` stimuli; Expected results = `then { }` / `Expected behaviour`. That makes the target artefact a *known schema with a normative grammar*, not a free-form document — strong support for the claim that one LLM call per coverage item is filling standardised slots rather than inventing a format.
- Both languages deliberately leave the leaf content as quoted free text and both state that no notation construct prevents nonsense (ES 202 553 clause 4.5). So the division of labour the tool uses — deterministic enumeration and structure, LLM for the quoted leaf text, rule-based verification afterwards — is the division the languages themselves presuppose. The rule-based verification stage is, in ETSI's terms, the "appropriate tools [that] may be able to identify such constructions".
- TDL Part 4's `PICSReference` list with and/or combination and a "not" negation is a published formal semantics for exactly the Technology/Profile gating in TS 18013-6, and for ETSI's warning that selection expressions "may be complex". A generator that emits coverage items without an applicability predicate is emitting objects of a strictly weaker type than the official suite's, which may be the largest single structural difference.
- TDL Part 4's "test objective variants … specialising or overriding data elements" is the published mechanism for the templating observed in the official suite (112 distinct precondition blocks across 907 cases, the commonest shared by 154). The classical answer to "why do 118 families average 7.7 cases" is: one structured test objective with variants, not 7.7 independent authoring acts.

### Gaps
- I found no published study measuring whether TPLan or TDL test purposes can be *generated* mechanically with measured accuracy, nor any LLM-based TPLan/TDL generator. ES 202 553 claims tool-checkability of syntax; I found no evaluation of semantic checking.
- ES 203 119-4's version status is unclear across sources: I read V1.5.1 (2022-05); search results indicated a V1.6.1 exists as "Final draft" on the ETSI portal. Cite V1.5.1, which I verified.

## Q5. Reported figures — test purposes per requirement, suite sizes, and completeness studies

### Takeaway
The only published per-requirement ratios I found are from ETSI's own IPv6 project, and they run the *opposite* way to intuition: roughly **0.15 to 0.32 conformance tests per catalogued requirement** (1112 requirements → 356 tests; 695 → 105; 1098 → 163). ETSI's methodology standard also fixes a size norm — "a test suite should never exceed a few hundreds of tests (e.g. 400 tests) no matter how complex the protocol is" — and both ETSI and ISO state flatly that no metric exists to assess a conformance suite's coverage of its standard.

### Cited Findings

Ratios, from the ETSI IPv6 project (the one documented case of running this pipeline over RFCs end to end):
- **Phase 1, IPv6 Core** — 7 RFCs (RFC 2460, 2373, 2461, 2462, 2463, 1981, 2675): "**1112 requirements / 356 validated conformance tests / 99 validated interoperability tests**" — [Randall, ITU-T workshop, 8 Dec 2006, slide 23](https://itu.int/dms_pub/itu-t/oth/06/02/T06020000020001PDFE.pdf)
- **Phase 2, IPsec** — 7 RFCs (4301, 4306, 4302, 4303, 2405, 2410, 4305): "**695 requirements / 105 conformance tests / 40 validated interoperability tests**" — same source, slide 24
- **Phase 2, Mobile IPv6** — 4 RFCs (3775, 3776, 2473, 4068): "**1098 requirements / 163 validated conformance tests / 100 validated interoperability tests**" — slide 25
- **Phase 2, IPv4→IPv6 transition** — 6 RFCs (2529, 2765, 2766, 3056, 4213, 4214): "**427 requirements**", conformance and interoperability specifications still in development at the time — slide 26
- The many-to-many relation that makes those ratios possible is stated explicitly for both conformance and interoperability TPs: "One Requirement may spawn several TPs / One TP may test several Requirements" — slides 10, 16

Suite-size and effort norms:
- "**A test suite should never exceed a few hundreds of tests (e.g. 400 tests) no matter how complex the protocol is.** This approach to the word 'reasonable' may sound rough, unjustified and arbitrary, but is often verified. The scheme proposed by TTCN practically limits the volume of tests contained in a test suite, if this test suite is to remain manageable. If more tests are needed, several test suites are advised. In this case, the reference 'protocol' may often be divided into several protocols." — [ETS 300 406, Annex D.3 Note 1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- "It is always possible to specify as many tests as one wants; the number of tests should be kept 'reasonable' … **The main issue is that no rigorous criterion can define the word 'reasonable'.**" — ETS 300 406 Annex D.3
- "the coverage reached by a test suite depends on one hand on the number of tests, on the other hand on the quality and pertinence of the tests. **Few pertinent tests have better value than many irrelevant tests** (for example, endlessly testing all the theoretically possible transitions of a state table, although many of them are practically impossible). The main effort … should be aimed at **increasing the pertinence** of the tests by choosing them carefully, **instead of at increasing the number** of tests produced" — ETS 300 406 Annex D.3
- "as the design of a test specification can hardly be estimated with respect to an intended coverage, the design of a test specification is generally thought in terms of **design-to-cost**" — ETS 300 406 Annex D.3

Completeness: the published position is that it is not measurable:
- "**The coverage of the conformance requirements of the reference specification by the TPs is never absolute nor complete, and no method exists to objectively evaluate it.** ISO/IEC 9646-2, subclause 10.4, gives only general guidance to evaluate it **subjectively**." — [ETS 300 406, clause 7.4.1.2](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_i_ets/300400_300499/300406/01_60/ets_300406e01p.pdf)
- "**Going from conformance requirements to TPs is an empirical job today. No metrics exist to assess the coverage of a test specification.** See ISO/IEC 9646-2, subclause 10.4." — ETS 300 406 Annex D.3
- What stands in for a metric is committee consensus: "the standardized TPs mean that **a consensus has been achieved in a standardization committee** … on the fact that the standardized TPs provide the desirable level of coverage of the reference specification. Furthermore, providing a **list of untestable TPs** is useful in order to document what is not covered by the ATS due to the choice of the ATM." — ETS 300 406 clause 7.4.1.2
- The operational definition of minimum coverage, where one exists (ETS 300 406 Annex D.3): "a minimum coverage could be defined by: the testing of **all basic capabilities** (Basic interconnection tests and capability tests); the coverage of the **optional items in the PICS**; the **parameter variations tests should exercise minimum and maximum values**"
- Explicit warning about formal/automatic derivation, written in 1995: "The only attempts to define coverage metrics are related to the use of formal description techniques. From a formal description … it is theoretically possible to determine a number of tests covering the totality of the protocol aspects. From this number, a coverage ratio may be computed … **In practice, this ratio is totally meaningless, because it does not take into account the fact that all the tests are not equally relevant. And precisely, the formal and automatic derivation of tests generally leads to a huge number of tests without interest.**" — ETS 300 406 Annex D.3 Note 2
- The same sentiment on systematic enumeration of invalid/inopportune cases: "It does not make sense to walk through all the 'empty boxes' of a state table. The tests of inopportune events may justify the use of a **state sampling** approach. **This makes all the difference between a systematic TP derivation from the protocol and an optimized TP design.**" — ETS 300 406 clause 7.4.1.1.3
- And on the unboundedness of negative testing: "The number of valid behaviour tests (variation tests excluded) is **limited and constrained by the complexity of the protocol**. However, the number of **invalid tests is, by nature almost unlimited**. The test specifier must determine the number of invalid and inopportune tests which is 'reasonable' … the selection of the 'invalid' tests to be included in the test suite should be made on the evaluation of the '**most probable defects**'" — [ETR 266 clause 4.4.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_etr/200_299/266/01_60/etr_266e01p.pdf)
- A qualitative balance criterion that is measurable in principle: "It is important to check that the '**density**' of test purposes is homogeneous for the different aspects of the protocol … the different roles or major functions of the protocol should be covered by an equivalent density of test purposes (with respect to the complexity level of each of these components). These elements related to the qualitative coverage are much more subjective … **Even if no metrics are available to measure this**, the problem should be carefully considered" — ETR 266 clause 4.4.1
- ISO/IEC 9646-2's own instruction to prefer a limited scope first: "Ideally, the TSS&TP should provide coverage of **all** of the conformance requirements … However, if the related ATS(s) are to be developed only to meet the needs of testing **particular profile(s)**, then the TSS&TP may initially be developed just to provide coverage for those conformance requirements … relevant to the particular profile(s). Such a TSS&TP **should be expanded to provide full coverage … when resources permit**." — [X.291 clause 10.4](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-X.291-199504-I!!PDF-E&type=items)

### Inferences
- Numerically, the comparison for the report: ETSI's IPv6 core produced **0.32 conformance tests per catalogued requirement** (356/1112); the tool produces **3.84 test cases per extracted requirement** (3231/842) — roughly a **12x** higher test-per-requirement density than the one published standards-body pipeline over natural-language input. The gap is not explained by the tool being thorough; ETSI deliberately deflates (combination TPs, factoring, state sampling, consensus pruning, design-to-cost) and ISO/IEC 9646-2 clause 10.3.3 prescribes the deflation as a formal second stage with supersession bookkeeping. The pipeline as described has the inflating half (one coverage item → one test case) and not the deflating half.
- A per-suite size check: the official suite's 907 cases sit above ETSI's "never exceed … e.g. 400" norm, consistent with it being a multi-part suite across several systems under test (three appendices, Technology-tagged by role); the tool's 3231 is ~8x the norm for a single suite and ~3.6x the official suite.
- The strongest citable claim for "what can be mechanised generically" is negative and comes from the standards bodies themselves: the *enumeration* is mechanisable (9646-2 clause 10.4 is a per-datatype rule set), the *selection* is not and is explicitly described as empirical, consensus-based and design-to-cost. A tool that mechanises enumeration and skips selection will, by ETS 300 406 Annex D.3 Note 2's 1995 prediction, produce "a huge number of tests without interest" — which is a precise, dated, citable prediction of a 13.8 % overlap outcome.

### Gaps
- I found **no published study measuring the completeness of a real conformance suite against its base standard** — no requirement-coverage audit of an ETSI, 3GPP or ISO suite. Both ETS 300 406 and X.291 say such a metric does not exist, which explains the absence but is not a substitute for a study. If the report needs one, this is a genuine hole in the literature and worth stating as such.
- No published figure for "test purposes per normative requirement" other than the ETSI IPv6 project numbers. The TS 18013-6 figures given in the brief (907 cases, 687 citing an 18013-5 clause, 118 families, 7.7 average) are, as far as my searches go, the only other such measurement — and they were measured by the user, not published.
- I did not find published post-mortems or case studies on building a conformance suite beyond the ETSI IPv6 slide deck. The deck is primary (ETSI TC-MTS vice-chair, at an ITU-T workshop) but it is a slide deck, so its numbers carry no methodology description.

## Q6. Published work automating any part of this chain, and what coverage it achieved

### Takeaway
Very little, and nothing that targets TSS&TP or ICS production from prose. ISO/IEC 9646-2 itself sanctions automated TP derivation but only from a *formal* description, and declares FDT methods out of scope. ETSI's answer is model-based testing (ES 202 951 + EG 203 130): a human builds a model from the requirements catalogue, and tests are generated from the model against a coverage criterion. The closest LLM-era work I found, iPanda (2025), generates executable test code from a specification plus an implementation and reports a Pass@1 multiple, not requirement coverage.

### Cited Findings
- The standard's own sanction and limit: "If the test suite specifier employs a formal description of the base specification(s) concerned, test purposes may be derived from that by means of **some automated method**. If an automated method is used, **the same requirements apply**. However, methods based on Formal Description Techniques (FDTs) are **outside the scope** of this Recommendation. Nevertheless, if an FDT is to be used for this purpose, it is preferred that it be a **standardized one**." — [X.291 clause 10.3.1 NOTE](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-X.291-199504-I!!PDF-E&type=items)
- ETSI's MBT route places automation after a human modelling step: "The MBT step '**Modelling for Testing**' is based on the standard and the corresponding requirements contained in the standard. As the modelling formalizes behaviour described in the standard and relates requirements to the model, **it can be seen as an additional validation step for the standard itself** and the requirements. Problems and ambiguities may [be] detected." — [EG 203 130 clause 5](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_eg/203100_203199/203130/01.01.01_60/eg_203130v010101p.pdf)
- Generation is driven by a declared coverage criterion, not by enumeration: the coverage criterion "chosen as a target for test generation is the main means of developer control over test generation process and the **main input to this process in addition to the model** used" and serves as "the **termination criterion** for the generator" — EG 203 130 clauses 5, 7.1
- Model quality attributes EG 203 130 requires of an MBT model include "**Traceability to standard requirements**: whether a model contains adequate references to standard requirements, and what part of model elements has such references" and "**Processability**: whether a model can be processed effectively by tools to generate necessary tests" — EG 203 130 clause 6.6; it then concedes "It is hard to provide general guidelines for objective measuring or achieving higher levels of these quality attributes"
- MSC/SDL-derived automation was evaluated in 1996 and found insufficient for TPs specifically: generated MSCs "do not fully cover the elements required for TPs. The main limitations are: 1) MSCs **do not include any definition of constraints** … 2) MSCs may define initial conditions, but this definition will be a **dynamic** one [leading] to the description of a large number of exchanges … 3) … it is possible to define alternative exchanges using MSCs but this will require additional MSC diagrams [making] verdict criteria much more complex than a prose one." Conclusion: "it is **difficult to conceive a schema in which the test purposes could be replaced by an MSC**. Essential components, such as the constraint information … or **reference to selection criteria**, are not covered." — [ETR 266 clauses 4.3.1.4.1 and 4.3.1.4.3](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_etr/200_299/266/01_60/etr_266e01p.pdf)
- Closest LLM-era work found: **iPanda**, an LLM-based agent for automated conformance testing of communication protocols. Per the search-result description of the paper, it takes a protocol specification plus an implementation, produces test cases by a keyword-based method, uses retrieval-augmented generation to write executable test code, and applies a self-correction loop; it reports raising **Pass@1 of test-code generation by roughly 4.7x to 10.8x** over pure-LLM baselines. The paper also notes specification-to-test-case translation is hard "because specifications are often ambiguous" and that LLMs struggle to map abstract rules onto concrete implementation APIs — [iPanda, arXiv:2507.00378 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00378). **Partially verified**: I read the search-result summary and the arXiv listing, not the full paper; the 4.7x–10.8x figure should be re-checked against the PDF before quoting.
- NIST **ICSGenerator** generates an implementation conformance statement, but from a formal model rather than prose: "At the heart of the generation process is an XML Schema file … created to describe the information model contained in the ISO/IEEE 11073-10201 Domain Information Model (DIM) standard"; the user loads an XML instance of that model and generation is driven by the selected Medical Device System object type — [NIST ICSGenerator User's Guide](https://www.nist.gov/document/icsgeneratorusersguide-v21doc)
- Tooling that reaches TTCN-3 does so from models, not prose: Conformiq's model-based tooling "automates test design, test data selection, and computation of expected results" with "generated TTCN-3 scripts"; PragmaDev RTDS generates TTCN-3 from message sequence charts; the TestUS framework derives a TTCN-3 suite from UML 2.0 use cases via a Markov-chain usage model — [PragmaDev Testing](https://www.pragmadev.com/product/testing.html); [Dulz, TestUS (SciTePress 2008)](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2008/17628/17628.pdf). **Unverified**: these are vendor pages and a short paper surfaced by search; I did not read them in full and no coverage figures were given.
- A 2015 paper "Automatic Generation of Test Cases and Test Purposes from Natural Language" (SBMF) was surfaced by search as the closest natural-language-input work, using a controlled natural language with a restricted lexicon and grammar; full text not retrieved — **unverified**, treat as a lead only.

### Inferences
- The chain's automatable boundary, as drawn by the standards themselves, sits in a specific place: requirements cataloguing is prescribed in enough detail to mechanise (EG 203 130 clause 6.1), coverage enumeration is prescribed in enough detail to mechanise (X.291 clause 10.4), and the two steps between them — ICS proforma construction and TSS design — have published *structural* rules (ETS 300 406 Annex A.2, X.291 clause 10.2) but no published automation. That middle pair is where a described pipeline that skips straight from requirements+objects to coverage items diverges most sharply from the tradition: the official suite's Technology/Profile tags and its 118-family taxonomy are precisely the outputs of those two skipped steps.
- ETR 266's 1996 verdict on MSC-based automation is directly transferable and quotable: the two things a generated artefact kept failing to supply were **constraints** (specific values to test) and **reference to selection criteria** (ICS applicability). Thirty years later those remain the two fields that distinguish the official 18013-6 record (Technology, Profile) from a bare coverage item.

### Gaps
- I found **no published work that generates a TSS&TP or an ICS proforma from a natural-language standard**, and therefore no coverage figure for such work. This appears to be a genuine gap in the literature rather than a gap in my search, but I searched in four phrasings only; the report should state it as "no published work found" rather than "none exists".
- The paper-search MCP server was not exercised (the brief flagged it as unreliable); all academic leads above came from web search and only iPanda was traced to a primary identifier.
